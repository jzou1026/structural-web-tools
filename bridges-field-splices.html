    Theory & Documentation

    This section documents the mechanics, limit-state equations, numerical workflow, and references used by the field-splice application. Equation numbers are cited in the Results interpretation and are retained in the printed report.

    Part I. Engineering Theory

    1. Girder cross-section properties

    \[\bar y=\frac{\sum_i A_i y_i}{\sum_i A_i} \tag{1}\]

    where \(\bar y\) is the elastic neutral-axis distance measured from the top of the girder, \(A_i\) is the area of component \(i\), and \(y_i\) is the distance from the top of the girder to the centroid of component \(i\). The components are the top flange, web, and bottom flange.

    \[I_x=\sum_i\left(I_{x,i}+A_i d_i^2\right),\qquad S_t=\frac{I_x}{\bar y},\qquad S_b=\frac{I_x}{D-\bar y} \tag{2}\]

    where \(I_x\) is the girder major-axis moment of inertia, \(I_{x,i}\) is the centroidal moment of inertia of component \(i\), \(d_i\) is the distance from the component centroid to the girder neutral axis, \(S_t\) and \(S_b\) are the elastic section moduli at the top and bottom fibres, and \(D\) is the total girder depth. Equation 2 is also used to determine the web contribution \(I_{x,w}\) about the girder neutral axis.

    2. Minimum splice design actions

    \[E_d=\max\!\left(E_{\mathrm{ULS}},\ 0.75R_{\mathrm{member}}\right) \tag{3}\]

    where \(E_d\) is the splice design action, \(E_{\mathrm{ULS}}\) is the calculated factored action at the splice, and \(R_{\mathrm{member}}\) is the factored resistance of the primary member for the same action. The application applies Equation 3 independently to bending moment, shear, and flange force in accordance with the source calculation and CSA S6 Clause 10.18.1.1.

    \[M_{0.75}=0.75\phi_s S_{\min}F_y \tag{4}\]

    where \(M_{0.75}\) is 75% of the factored girder moment resistance, \(\phi_s\) is the steel resistance factor, \(S_{\min}\) is the smaller applicable elastic section modulus, and \(F_y\) is the steel yield strength.

    3. Girder web shear resistance

    \[k_v=\begin{cases}4+\dfrac{5.34}{(a/h)^2},&a/h<1\\[4pt]5.34+\dfrac{4}{(a/h)^2},&a/h\ge1\end{cases} \tag{5}\]

    where \(k_v\) is the web shear-buckling coefficient, \(a\) is the transverse-stiffener spacing, and \(h\) is the clear web depth.

    \[F_s=F_{cr}+F_t, \qquad V_r=\phi_s A_wF_s, \qquad A_w=hw \tag{6}\]

    where \(F_s\) is the ultimate web shear stress, \(F_{cr}\) is the elastic or inelastic shear-buckling stress selected from the CSA S6 web-slenderness ranges, \(F_t\) is the permitted tension-field contribution, \(V_r\) is the factored girder shear resistance, \(A_w\) is the web shear area, and \(w\) is the web thickness. The design shear is obtained from Equation 3 using \(0.75V_r\).

    4. Flange forces and fatigue force ranges

    \[P_f=\frac{|M|}{S_f}A_f \tag{7}\]

    where \(P_f\) is the axial force in the selected flange, \(M\) is the bending moment for the applicable limit state and sign, \(S_f\) is the elastic section modulus associated with that flange and load stage, and \(A_f\) is the flange gross area. The application evaluates positive and negative moments and retains the governing force.

    \[\Delta P_f=A_f\left(\frac{|M_{FLS,+}|}{S_{f,+}}+\frac{|M_{FLS,-}|}{S_{f,-}}\right) \tag{8}\]

    where \(\Delta P_f\) is the flange force range, \(M_{FLS,+}\) and \(M_{FLS,-}\) are the positive and negative fatigue moments, and \(S_{f,+}\) and \(S_{f,-}\) are the corresponding elastic section moduli. The algebraic stress range is represented by the sum of the absolute positive- and negative-moment stress contributions.

    5. Web share of bending and splice eccentricity

    \[M_w=M_d\frac{I_{x,w}}{I_x},\qquad M_s=M_w+V_de \tag{9}\]

    where \(M_w\) is the portion of the girder design moment assigned to the web, \(M_d\) is the governing design moment from Equation 3, \(I_{x,w}\) is the web moment of inertia about the girder neutral axis, \(I_x\) is the full girder moment of inertia, \(M_s\) is the web-splice design moment, \(V_d\) is the governing design shear, and \(e\) is the horizontal distance between the splice centreline and the centroid of one web-bolt group.

    6. Web splice-plate resistance

    \[V_{r,g}=\phi_sA_{g,v}(0.577F_y) \tag{10}\]

    where \(V_{r,g}\) is the gross-section shear-yielding resistance of the two web splice plates and \(A_{g,v}\) is their combined gross shear area.

    \[V_{r,n}=\phi_u(0.6)A_{g,v}\frac{F_u+F_y}{2} \tag{11}\]

    where \(V_{r,n}\) is the source-calculation shear-fracture resistance, \(\phi_u\) is the fracture resistance factor, and \(F_u\) is the plate ultimate strength. The expression follows the shear-only block-shear treatment used in the supplied example.

    \[I_{sp}=\sum\left(I_i+A_i d_i^2\right),\qquad S_{sp}=\frac{I_{sp}}{c_{sp}},\qquad M_{r,sp}=\phi_sS_{sp}F_y \tag{12}\]

    where \(I_{sp}\) is the splice-plate moment of inertia about the girder neutral axis, \(S_{sp}\) is the splice-plate elastic section modulus, \(c_{sp}\) is the distance from the girder neutral axis to the governing splice-plate edge, and \(M_{r,sp}\) is the splice-plate moment resistance.

    7. ULS web bolt group

    \[J_b=\sum_i r_i^2=I_{x,b}+I_{y,b}+N d_n^2 \tag{13}\]

    where \(J_b\) is the polar coordinate sum of one bolt group about the girder neutral axis, \(r_i\) is the radial distance to bolt \(i\), \(I_{x,b}\) and \(I_{y,b}\) are the coordinate sums about the bolt-group centroidal axes, \(N\) is the number of bolts on one side of the splice, and \(d_n\) is the offset between the bolt-group centroid and the girder neutral axis.

    \[P_m=\frac{M_s r}{J_b},\qquad P_x=P_m\frac{y}{r},\qquad P_y=P_m\frac{x}{r} \tag{14}\]

    where \(P_m\) is the tangential force on the critical bolt caused by splice moment, \(r\) is the critical bolt radius, \(P_x\) and \(P_y\) are the horizontal and vertical components, and \(x\) and \(y\) are the critical bolt coordinates relative to the applicable centre of rotation.

    \[P_r=\sqrt{\left(\frac{V_d}{N}+P_y\right)^2+P_x^2} \tag{15}\]

    where \(P_r\) is the resultant ULS force on the critical web bolt and \(V_d/N\) is the direct-shear component per bolt.

    8. Bolt bearing, shear, and slip resistance

    \[B_r=3\phi_{br}ntd_bF_u \tag{16}\]

    where \(B_r\) is the plate bearing resistance, \(\phi_{br}\) is the bearing resistance factor, \(n\) is the number of bolts represented by the check, \(t\) is the connected plate thickness, and \(d_b\) is the bolt diameter.

    \[V_{r,b}=0.60\phi_{br}nmA_bF_{ub},\qquad A_b=\frac{\pi d_b^2}{4} \tag{17}\]

    where \(V_{r,b}\) is the factored bolt shear resistance, \(m\) is the number of shear planes, \(A_b\) is the nominal bolt area, and \(F_{ub}\) is the bolt ultimate strength. When threads intercept a shear plane, the application applies the Clause 10 reduction selected by the user.

    \[V_s=0.53C_sk_snmA_bF_{ub} \tag{18}\]

    where \(V_s\) is the slip resistance, \(C_s\) is the installation and resistance factor, and \(k_s\) is the mean slip coefficient for the faying-surface class. The SLS critical web-bolt resultant combines direct shear, web-bending force, and the force caused by shear eccentricity before comparison with Equation 18.

    9. Flange and splice-plate tension resistance

    \[T_{r,g}=\phi_sA_gF_y,\qquad T_{r,n}=\phi_uA_nF_u \tag{19}\]

    where \(T_{r,g}\) is gross-section yielding resistance, \(T_{r,n}\) is net-section fracture resistance, \(A_g\) is gross area, and \(A_n\) is gross area less the design-hole deductions across the critical transverse path.

    \[T_{r,bs}=\phi_{bs}\left(U_tA_{nt}F_u+0.6A_{gv}F_{m}\right),\qquad F_m=\frac{F_u+F_y}{2} \tag{20}\]

    where \(T_{r,bs}\) is block-shear resistance, \(\phi_{bs}\) is the source-calculation block-shear factor, \(U_t\) is the tension-plane efficiency factor, \(A_{nt}\) is the net tension area, \(A_{gv}\) is the gross shear area, and \(F_m\) is the average of ultimate and yield strengths. The bottom- and top-flange paths follow the dimensions used in the supplied calculation.

    10. Unequal flange splice-plate force distribution

    \[P_i=\frac{z_o}{z_i+z_o}P_d,\qquad P_o=\frac{z_i}{z_i+z_o}P_d \tag{21}\]

    where \(P_i\) and \(P_o\) are the inside- and outside-plate forces, \(P_d\) is the flange splice design force, and \(z_i\) and \(z_o\) are the force-line lever arms obtained from equilibrium of the eccentric splice assembly. For the source bottom-flange arrangement, the application uses the source distribution of 0.59 to the inside plates and a conservative 0.50 to the outside plate.

    11. Filler and long-joint effects

    \[R_f=\max\left[0.85,\ 1-0.0158\left(t'-6.4\right)\right] \tag{22}\]

    where \(R_f\) is the bolt shear reduction factor and \(t'\) is the total filler thickness in millimetres. The reduced bolt shear resistance is \(R_fV_{r,b}\). The application also flags shear-transfer lengths exceeding 760 mm for the long-joint provision.

    12. Fatigue resistance and stress-range checks

    \[F_{sr}=\max\left[\left(\frac{\gamma}{N_c}\right)^{1/3},\ \left(\frac{\gamma'}{N_c}\right)^{1/5},\ \frac{F_{srt}}{2}\right] \tag{23}\]

    where \(F_{sr}\) is the fatigue stress-range resistance, \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\) are the Category B fatigue-life constants, \(N_c\) is the number of design cycles, and \(F_{srt}\) is the constant-amplitude threshold stress range.

    \[0.52C_Lf_{sr}\le F_{sr},\qquad f_{sr}=\frac{\Delta P}{A_{sp}} \tag{24}\]

    where \(C_L\) is the fatigue load factor, \(f_{sr}\) is the calculated splice-plate stress range, \(\Delta P\) is the force range assigned to the splice plate, and \(A_{sp}\) is its gross area. The application checks the inside and outside plates separately.

    Part II. Coding Theory

    1. Application architecture

    The application is a single-file HTML program containing semantic HTML, a shared CSS design system, inline SVG graphics, and plain JavaScript. No server, database, build system, or external numerical library is required. MathJax is the only runtime dependency and is loaded from the jsDelivr CDN.

    2. Input and state processing

    Each editable field has a unique DOM identifier. The script reads the current values with a numeric accessor, calculates dependent geometry and material values once, and passes those values through a deterministic sequence of section-property, member-resistance, plate-resistance, bolt-group, slip, and fatigue calculations. Girder geometry and material properties are not duplicated.

    3. Calculation pipeline

    1. Read and normalize girder, splice, bolt, and limit-state inputs.
    2. Calculate the girder centroid, \(I_x\), section moduli, web inertia, bolt area, and standard/design hole sizes.
    3. Calculate member moment and shear resistance and apply the 75% primary-member minimum through Equation 3.
    4. Convert moments to flange forces through Equations 7 and 8.
    5. Calculate web splice-plate and bolt-group demands using Equations 9 through 18.
    6. Calculate flange, splice-plate, block-shear, filler, slip, and fatigue checks using Equations 19 through 24.
    7. Render results tables, utilization graphics, status labels, interpretation notes, and the report metadata.

    4. Working-file and print workflows

    The working-file command clones the current document, writes current form values into the cloned HTML, and downloads a standalone session file. The print command first awaits the MathJax startup and typesetting promises, then pauses briefly before opening the browser print dialog. Print CSS preserves equation blocks, tables, headings, and the Theory & Documentation section without rasterizing the equations.

    5. Numerical precision and source reproduction

    Calculations retain full JavaScript floating-point precision. Display values are rounded only when rendered. Therefore, small differences from the printed source can occur where the source rounds bolt diameter, bolt area, gross area, neutral-axis position, moment of inertia, or section modulus before the next step.
