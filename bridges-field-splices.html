    Clause 10 Method

    1. Minimum connection design action

    \[E_d=\max(E_{calc},0.75R_{member})\]

    CSA S6:25 Clause 10.18.1.1 requires primary-member splices to resist the larger of calculated effects and 75% of the factored member resistance.

    2. Web share of moment

    \[M_{web}=M_d\frac{I_{web}}{I_{steel}},\qquad M_s=M_{web}+V_de\]

    The tool distributes girder moment to the web in proportion to elastic moment of inertia, then adds the eccentric moment caused by shear between the splice axis and one bolt-group centroid.

    3. Web plate resistance

    \[V_r=\phi_sA_g(0.577F_y),\qquad M_r=\phi_sS_{sp}F_y\]

    The gross shear yielding calculation follows Clause 10.10.5.1. The elastic splice-plate moment resistance is checked separately.

    4. Bolts

    \[B_r=3\phi_{br}ntdF_u,\qquad V_r=0.60\phi_{br}nmA_bF_{ub}\]
    \[V_s=0.53C_sk_snmA_bF_{ub}\]

    Bearing and bolt shear are checked at ULS under Clause 10.18.2.3.3. Slip resistance is checked at service load levels under Clause 10.18.2.3.2. Threads intercepted by a shear plane reduce ULS bolt shear resistance to 0.7 of the calculated value.

    5. Filler reduction

    \[R_f=1-0.0158(t'-6.4)\ge 0.85\]

    The current Clause 10.18.4.12 expression is used when filler thickness exceeds 6.4 mm. For a 13 mm filler, the factor is approximately 0.896.

    6. Fatigue

    \[0.52C_Lf_{sr}\le F_{sr}\]

    Mechanically fastened, high-strength slip-critical connections with drilled or punched-and-reamed holes are treated as Category B in Table 10.7. The default input uses a fatigue stress-range resistance of 55.2 MPa; the engineer must confirm the applicable value for the selected detail category and cycle count.
