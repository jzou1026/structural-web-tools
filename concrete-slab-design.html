Theory & Documentation

Part 1: Engineering Theory

1. Section idealization and sign convention

The application models a rectangular reinforced-concrete slab strip of width b and thickness h. The top and bottom reinforcement are concentrated into discrete layers at their centroidal depths. Axial force P is positive in compression. Positive moment M places the bottom reinforcement in tension.

The analysis assumes plane sections remain plane, perfect bond exists between concrete and reinforcement, concrete tension is neglected at resistance, and reinforcing steel follows an elastic-perfectly-plastic stress-strain relationship. Strength reduction factors are applied directly to the concrete and steel force resultants.

2. Reinforcement geometry

ds,top=ctop+db,top2,ds,bot=h−cbot−db,bot2Equation 1

where ds,top and ds,bot are the reinforcement centroid depths measured from the top face, ctop and cbot are clear covers, db is bar diameter, and h is slab thickness.

3. Linear strain compatibility

For a selected compression face and neutral-axis depth c, the extreme concrete compression strain is fixed at εcu. By the plane-sections assumption, strain varies linearly over the section depth:

εs(d)=εcu(1−dc)Equation 2

where εs(d) is the longitudinal strain at a reinforcement layer, εcu is the limiting concrete compression strain, d is the distance from the active compression face to that layer, and c is the distance from the active compression face to the neutral axis. Compression strain is positive. A layer located beyond the neutral axis has d>c, so Equation 2 returns a negative tensile strain.

The yield strain is obtained from Hooke's law:

εy=fyEsEquation 3

where εy is steel yield strain, fy is steel yield strength, and Es is the steel modulus of elasticity.

4. Balanced condition

The balanced condition occurs when the extreme concrete compression strain reaches εcu while the reinforcement farthest from the compression face reaches tensile yield, −εy. Similar triangles applied to the linear strain diagram give:

cbdt=εcuεcu+εyEquation 4
cb=εcuεcu+εydtEquation 5

where cb is the balanced neutral-axis depth and dt is the distance from the active compression face to the centroid of the farthest tension reinforcement. Equations 4 and 5 are used in the Balanced tab.

After cb is known, Equation 2 is applied separately to every reinforcement layer. This is important when the reinforcement is asymmetric because the compression layer may remain elastic, reach compression yield, or lie in tension depending on its position relative to cb.

5. Steel constitutive relationship

fs=clip(Esεs,−fy,+fy)Equation 6
Fs=ϕsfsAsEquation 7

where fs is reinforcement stress, clip(⋅) limits elastic stress to the interval from −fy to +fy, Fs is the factored reinforcement force, ϕs is the steel resistance factor, and As is the area of the reinforcement layer. A positive Fs is compression and a negative Fs is tension.

6. Concrete compression block

Concrete tension is omitted. Concrete compression is represented by an equivalent rectangular stress block with stress α1ϕcfc′ and depth a=β1c:

a=min(h,β1c)Equation 8
Cc=ϕcα1fc′baEquation 9

where a is equivalent compression-block depth, α1 is the concrete stress coefficient, β1 is the block-depth coefficient, ϕc is the concrete resistance factor, fc′ is specified concrete compressive strength, b is strip width, and Cc is the concrete compression resultant. The resultant acts at a/2 from the active compression face.

7. Axial and moment equilibrium

Pr=Cc+∑iFs,iEquation 10
Mr=Ccyc+∑iFs,iyiEquation 11

where Pr is axial resistance, Mr is moment resistance about the section centroid, yc is the signed lever arm from the section centroid to the concrete resultant, and yi is the signed lever arm from the section centroid to reinforcement layer i. The balanced eccentricity is then:

eb=|MbPb|Equation 12

where eb is balanced eccentricity, Mb is balanced moment resistance, and Pb is balanced axial resistance.

8. Interaction envelope and demand check

The script sweeps c from both compression faces. Equations 2 and 6 through 11 generate one (Mr,Pr) point for each neutral-axis depth. Pure tension and uniform-compression endpoints are added, and a monotone-chain convex-hull algorithm forms a closed resistance envelope.

For each demand point (Mf,Pf), the program casts a ray from the origin through the demand and intersects that ray with every envelope segment. If the nearest positive intersection is λ(Mf,Pf), the radial utilization is:

U=1λEquation 13

where U is the demand-to-capacity ratio and λ is the radial capacity scale factor. The displayed check is acceptable when U≤1.0. This radial check is a computational envelope check, not a substitute for all code-required slab verifications.

Part 2: Coding Theory

Data flow: input controls → state object → geometry and material validation → reinforcement-layer generation → neutral-axis sweep → force and moment equilibrium → convex resistance envelope → radial demand checks → Plotly and MathJax rendering.

Program architecture

  • State and binding: A central JavaScript object stores material properties, section dimensions, reinforcement, and load cases. Input event handlers update this object and redraw the section preview.
  • Geometry model: The layers() function converts bar size, clear cover, and steel area per metre into top and bottom reinforcement fibres with section-centroid coordinates.
  • Section solver: The point(face,c) function evaluates one neutral-axis state using Equations 2 and 6 through 11.
  • Envelope builder: The solver sweeps neutral-axis depth from both faces, adds axial endpoints, and applies a monotone-chain convex hull to remove interior points and order the boundary.
  • Demand evaluation: The rayCapacity() routine computes line-segment intersections and returns λ and U from Equation 13.
  • Balanced solver: The balancedCalc() routine first obtains cb from Equation 5 and then performs the same force and moment equilibrium used by the interaction solver.
  • Presentation: SVG provides the section and strain diagrams, Plotly renders interactive P–M graphs, and MathJax converts live LaTeX into scalable CHTML for screen and print.
  • Persistence: “Save Working File” serializes the current state into the HTML file so that reopening the saved copy restores all editable inputs.

Validation and limitations

The script checks positive material and geometric inputs and verifies that top and bottom reinforcement layers do not overlap. The application does not independently verify punching shear, one-way shear, deflection, crack control, development length, minimum reinforcement, diaphragm action, second-order effects, load combinations, or complete code compliance.
