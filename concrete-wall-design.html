Theory & Documentation

Part 1: Engineering Theory

1. Section idealization

The wall is analyzed as a rectangular horizontal section in plan with length \(l_w\) and thickness \(t_w\). Vertical reinforcement is represented by discrete steel fibres at coordinates \(x_i\) measured from the wall centroid. Concrete tension is neglected. Compression axial force is positive, and the reported moment sign follows the selected compression edge.

The model assumes plane sections remain plane, perfect bond between concrete and reinforcement, an elastic-perfectly-plastic reinforcing-steel response, and an equivalent rectangular concrete compression block.

2. Distributed and boundary reinforcement

\[s_b=\frac{A_b}{A_{s,r}/1000}\]Equation 1

where \(s_b\) is equivalent bar spacing in millimetres, \(A_b\) is the area of one reinforcing bar in square millimetres, and \(A_{s,r}\) is the entered reinforcement area rate in square millimetres per metre. Distributed fibres are placed between the cover limits. Additional boundary bars are placed uniformly within each selected end zone.

3. Linear strain compatibility

For a selected compression edge and neutral-axis depth \(c\), the extreme concrete compression strain is fixed at \(\varepsilon_{cu}\). The strain at any steel fibre follows from similar triangles:

\[\varepsilon_{s,i}=\varepsilon_{cu}\left(1-\frac{d_i}{c}\right)\]Equation 2

where \(\varepsilon_{s,i}\) is strain in steel fibre \(i\), \(\varepsilon_{cu}\) is the limiting concrete compression strain, \(d_i\) is the distance from the active compression edge to fibre \(i\), and \(c\) is the neutral-axis depth from that edge. Fibres with \(d_i>c\) have negative tensile strain.

\[\varepsilon_y=\frac{f_y}{E_s}\]Equation 3

where \(\varepsilon_y\) is steel yield strain, \(f_y\) is steel yield strength, and \(E_s\) is the steel modulus of elasticity.

4. Balanced condition

The balanced condition occurs when concrete reaches \(\varepsilon_{cu}\) while the farthest tension reinforcement reaches \(-\varepsilon_y\). Similar triangles give:

\[\frac{c_b}{d_t}=\frac{\varepsilon_{cu}}{\varepsilon_{cu}+\varepsilon_y}\]Equation 4
\[c_b=\frac{\varepsilon_{cu}}{\varepsilon_{cu}+\varepsilon_y}\,d_t\]Equation 5

where \(c_b\) is the balanced neutral-axis depth and \(d_t\) is the distance from the active compression edge to the farthest tension reinforcing fibre. After \(c_b\) is found, Equation 2 is applied to every distributed and boundary fibre independently.

5. Steel stress and force

\[f_{s,i}=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(E_s\varepsilon_{s,i},-f_y,+f_y\right)\]Equation 6
\[F_{s,i}=\phi_s f_{s,i}A_{s,i}\]Equation 7

where \(f_{s,i}\) is stress in fibre \(i\), \(\operatorname{clip}(\cdot)\) limits stress to the yield bounds, \(F_{s,i}\) is factored fibre force, \(\phi_s\) is the steel resistance factor, and \(A_{s,i}\) is fibre area. Compression is positive and tension is negative.

6. Equivalent concrete compression block

\[a=\min\!\left(l_w,\beta_1c\right)\]Equation 8
\[C_c=\phi_c\alpha_1 f'_c t_w a\]Equation 9

where \(a\) is compression-block depth, \(\alpha_1\) is the concrete stress coefficient, \(\beta_1\) is the block-depth coefficient, \(\phi_c\) is the concrete resistance factor, \(f'_c\) is specified concrete compressive strength, \(t_w\) is wall thickness, and \(C_c\) is the concrete compression resultant. The resultant acts at \(a/2\) from the active compression edge.

7. Axial and moment equilibrium

\[P_r=C_c+\sum_iF_{s,i}\]Equation 10
\[M_r=-C_cx_c-\sum_iF_{s,i}x_i\]Equation 11

where \(P_r\) is axial resistance, \(M_r\) is moment resistance about the wall centroid, \(x_c\) is the signed coordinate of the concrete resultant, and \(x_i\) is the signed coordinate of fibre \(i\). The balanced eccentricity is:

\[e_b=\left|\frac{M_b}{P_b}\right|\]Equation 12

where \(e_b\) is balanced eccentricity, \(M_b\) is balanced moment resistance, and \(P_b\) is balanced axial resistance.

8. Resistance conventions

Factored \(M_r\): uses the entered \(\phi_c\) and \(\phi_s\). Nominal \(M_n\): sets both factors to 1.0. Probable \(M_p\): sets both factors to 1.0 and uses \(f_{y,\mathrm{prob}}=1.25f_y\). The probable-strength multiplier is an application assumption that must be checked against the governing project criteria.

9. Interaction envelope and demand utilization

The solver sweeps \(c\) from both wall ends. Equations 2 and 6 through 11 produce one \((M_r,P_r)\) point for each neutral-axis depth. Pure tension and compression endpoints are added, and a monotone-chain convex hull creates the closed envelope.

\[U=\frac{1}{\lambda}\]Equation 13

where \(U\) is the radial demand-to-capacity ratio and \(\lambda\) is the positive scale factor at which the ray from the origin through demand \((M_f,P_f)\) intersects the resistance envelope. The displayed section check is acceptable when \(U\leq1.0\).

Part 2: Coding Theory

Data flow: input controls → application state → SI conversion and validation → fibre generation → neutral-axis sweep → section equilibrium → convex resistance envelope → demand checks → Plotly and MathJax output.

  • State and units: A central JavaScript object stores geometry, materials, arrangements, resistance convention, and demands. The si() function converts metric or imperial display values to internal newton-millimetre units.
  • Fibre model: bars() creates distributed and boundary steel fibres with area and centroid coordinates.
  • Section solver: point(face,c,st,A) evaluates Equations 2 and 6 through 11 for one neutral-axis state.
  • Envelope: envelope() sweeps neutral-axis depth from both ends and hull() orders the convex resistance boundary.
  • Demand check: radial() intersects the demand ray with every envelope segment and calculates Equation 13.
  • Balanced solver: balancedCalc() obtains \(c_b\) from Equation 5, then evaluates all concrete and steel resultants using the same equilibrium engine.
  • Presentation: SVG renders the wall and strain diagrams, Plotly renders interaction graphs, and MathJax renders live LaTeX as scalable CHTML.
  • Persistence: Save Working File serializes the current state into a standalone HTML copy.

Scope limitation

The application is a section-strength tool. It does not complete wall-system analysis, shear design, stability, second-order effects, coupling-beam design, boundary-element qualification, confinement, anchorage, development, seismic capacity design, foundation checks, or complete code compliance.
